# Marketing Consent and Messaging Policy

## Baseline

An inbound one-to-one message permits relevant service replies to that conversation; it is not blanket marketing consent. A status view, inferred interest, group membership, saved phone number, prior purchase, or silence is not consent. Marketing automation requires a separate affirmative choice with evidence.

## Valid evidence

Record who consented, the identity/channel, date/time, exact scope, wording shown or spoken, source, collector, policy version, and supporting reference. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, and separable from unnecessary terms. Pre-checked boxes and bundled consent are prohibited.

## Sending rules

- Identify the business and keep content within the recorded scope.
- Provide a simple opt-out in marketing messages where appropriate.
- Apply suppression before scheduling or sending, not after delivery.
- Do not upload or use bought, scraped, shared, or stale lists without a documented lawful basis and consent review.
- Respect provider policies, quiet hours, frequency limits, vulnerable recipients, and consumer-protection rules.

## Withdrawal and STOP

STOP, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL MESSAGES, OPT OUT, DON'T MESSAGE ME, and equivalent clear language immediately create a withdrawal/suppression record and prevent automated replies. Do not send a marketing confirmation that defeats the opt-out. A service response may be sent only where necessary and lawful. Re-enrolment requires a new affirmative opt-in; staff may not simply clear suppression.

## Governance

Marketing consent records are immutable events; the latest state controls. Sample records quarterly, investigate complaints, disable non-compliant campaigns, and retain evidence according to the approved retention schedule. Status-view and audience features must remain operationally separate from consent.
